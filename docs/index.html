Money should represent a fixed quantity of work, not a fixed quantity of money. Fix the work and let the supply float, and efficiency stops being a transfer from the productive to the passive.

A fixed supply means every productivity gain is captured by whoever already holds money. Produce twice as efficiently and competition forces you to sell cheaper; the surplus lands in the purchasing power of existing holders, not in your pocket. Efficiency becomes a subsidy from workers to hoarders. Scrip fixes the work per token instead, forever, so becoming twice as efficient earns you tokens twice as fast without taking anything from anyone. Each token is still worth exactly the work it cost. Nobody's work is confiscated, and nobody is paid for holding. Your money will not gain value by you doing nothing.

Emission is set by arithmetic, not policy. A hash with k leading zero bits appears with probability 2-k-1 and pays 2k-40 tokens, so every rarity level is worth exactly the same and the whole ladder sums to 44.5 · 2⁻⁴⁰ tokens per hash. Difficulty never retargets, so a miner with twice the hashrate mints twice as much permanently: competition shows up in price instead of in a difficulty adjustment, and early work keeps a surplus that decays only as supply accumulates. And because every token costs electricity and hardware to produce, its price is anchored to joules and tends toward an industrial commodity rather than a discretionary issuance schedule.
